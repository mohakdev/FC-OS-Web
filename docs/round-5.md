# Round 5: slide content

Founders Club, SRM DEI. Slide copy only, one line a bullet.

---

## 1. Innovation Mission Map overview

- Founders Club is the official student body of the SRM Directorate of Entrepreneurship and Innovation.
- We exist to resolve, ignite, innovate, inspire, and build community.
- By 2030 we want to be one of South India's leading student founder networks.
- This year: startup workshops, connecting students to founders, bootcamps.
- What holds us back today is risk aversion, low awareness, and ideas that repeat.
- Our answer to the emerging technology question was to build our own digital infrastructure first, then take it toward generative AI and agents.

---

## 2. How the IMM was followed

- Box 1 of the map is the one we actually executed. FC OS is that box, built and running on our own hardware.
- The present challenges shaped it. Nobody knew who owned what, so work is now tracked per domain with a real owner and a real status.
- Our core strengths made it possible. Our own committee wrote it and our own machine runs it, so there was no budget to ask for.
- The collaboration box is why access is permission based. Alumni, advisors and partners can see the club without being able to change it.
- Our impact measure stays honest because contribution and attendance sit in a database instead of in somebody's memory.
- The map was not a form we filled after the fact. It is the order we built in.

---

## 3. Signature innovation overview

- Our bet is that within a few years every member walks in with an agent already running for them.
- So we are shaping FC OS as something an agent can operate, not only a screen a human clicks.
- An MCP server exposes club operations as typed, permission checked calls.
- An agent inherits its owner's scope and nothing past it. It cannot do what its owner cannot do.
- A human still approves anything that writes.
- The work we hand over first: draft the minutes, file the tasks, chase the ones that stall, queue content for FC TV.
- One agent at the club level can answer what the state of the club is right now, from live data.

---

## 4. Mapping the signature innovation to our vision

- The vision is a leading student founder network by 2030. A network that size cannot be run by hand.
- Agents take the admin load off the committee, so people spend their hours on founders instead of follow ups.
- Roles, domains and permissions are rows in a database, so a second club or a second campus is a scoping change and not a rewrite.
- What the committee knows stops leaving with the committee, which is what lets a network compound instead of resetting every year.

---

## 5. Improvements after crisis adaptation

- Our sponsor asked us to make this usable by rural communities.
- Self hosting already fit that. One machine, no per seat cost, no cloud bill, and it already runs on a campus network that blocks most tunnels.
- We are adding regional language support, including right to left layouts, through the component library we already build on.
- We are tuning for low bandwidth and older phones. The pages are server rendered and light to begin with.
- The permission grid stays editable, so a community names its own roles instead of inheriting ours.
- Nothing here is a separate build. It is the same app with the assumptions loosened.

---

## 6. Implementation model for crisis adaptation

- Same system, different institution. A school runs it the way a club does.
- Teachers plan lessons on a board instead of tasks, with the same owners, statuses and deadlines.
- Attendance is recorded per class, and performance is scored from what was actually done.
- Role based access means a student who missed a day can open that day and see what was covered.
- Parents and visiting staff get read only access and cannot touch anything.
- One machine in the school office serves the whole campus, with nightly encrypted backups.
- Regional languages and RTL make it usable where English is not the first language in the room.

---

## 7. Next year execution plan

- Attendance interface first. The tables already exist, the screen does not.
- MoM with transcription, so meeting audio produces action items that file themselves as tasks.
- Performance tracker, scoring contribution from Opus and attendance data.
- FC TV CMS for the club screens.
- The MCP layer and the first member agents, with humans approving every write.
- Ops work: cluster mode for throughput, a second box for redundancy, a UPS.
- Open source the codebase so other student clubs can run their own.

---

## 8. Expected impact

| Short term | Long term |
|---|---|
| Approval is a queue someone clears, not a favour someone remembers | Decisions, tasks and attendance survive across tenures |
| Access follows the role, so a member who steps down loses write access that moment | Each committee starts from what the last one knew |
| End of term reviews rest on a record, not on who remembers whom | Members graduate having run production software, not having demoed a prototype |
| Work is visible per domain, with an owner and a status | Any club or school can host the same thing for the price of one machine |

---

## 9. Sustainability and future expansion

- No recurring cost. Our hardware, our database, our backups.
- Nothing to migrate off later, and no vendor that can price us out of our own operations.
- The club maintains it, and handover is part of the system rather than a favour the last team does.
- Expansion path: a second club on the same deployment, then schools and community organisations.
- Open sourcing turns every adopter into a maintainer, which is how it outlives any one committee.
